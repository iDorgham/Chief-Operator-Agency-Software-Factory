# Social Media Operating System

## Architecture

Social media is divided into four controlled layers:

1. **Strategy:** business objective, audience, positioning, channel roles, content pillars, narrative, offers and measurement definitions.
2. **Plan:** campaigns, editorial calendar, formats, cadence, production capacity, dependencies, experiments and approval dates.
3. **Management:** intake, content queue, asset production, review, scheduling, community response, issue escalation, analytics and retrospective.
4. **Automation:** approved scheduling, reminders, monitoring, classification, routing, reporting and reusable content transformations with pause, rate, error and audit controls.

## Ownership

- Marketing and Growth Lead owns strategy, plan, calendar, community policy and performance interpretation.
- Design and Content Lead owns brand-consistent copy, image, video and asset provenance.
- Operations Lead owns metric definitions, source quality, reporting and improvement evidence.
- Software Lead owns technical integrations and automation reliability.
- Chief Operator presents exact publish, reply, spend and automation approvals.

## Workflow

Objective -> research/evidence -> channel architecture -> content pillars -> campaign/editorial plan -> briefs -> text/image/video production -> claim/brand/accessibility review -> approval -> scheduling/publishing -> community management -> measurement -> retrospective.

## Automation boundary

Safe unattended work may collect authorized metrics, identify draft opportunities, classify incoming items, prepare reports and remind owners. Public posting, public replies, moderation with reputation impact, advertising spend and material campaign changes remain approval-gated unless the operator has approved an exact bounded policy.

Every automation defines account/channel, input, output, cadence, permissions, rate limits, deduplication, approval step, failure state, pause/kill switch, audit record and recovery procedure.

## Required artifacts

- Social-media strategy and channel architecture.
- Editorial/campaign plan and calendar.
- Content and creative briefs.
- Community-management and escalation policy.
- Automation specification and approval matrix.
- Source-grounded analytics report and retrospective.
